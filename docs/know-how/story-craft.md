# Story craft — the operational checklists

Apply these whenever story material is written or revised: pitches, chapter cards, outlines, scene lists, beats, character arcs, mid-story rewrites, ad-hoc brainstorms. The theory is in `docs/craft/intention-obstacle.md`; this makes it enforceable. Run every check before presenting to the author. Fix or flag failures — never ship one silently.

## Before drafting — load the frame

Read `stories/<slug>/story.md` (theme, feel line, topology, curves, braid mechanisms), the relevant cast files, and the neighbouring storyboard cards or chapter plans. If no pitch is locked yet, the curve and braid checks run against the pitch being developed.

## Check 1 — the one-sentence test

Every unit, every altitude:

> *[Character] intends to [X], but [Y] stands in the way.*

Specific and storyboardable, with a clock where possible. "Wants to be loved" is DNA, not an intent.

## Check 2 — press it

- **The intent:** why would *this* character want this? It traces to a Desire, through a Shadow.
- **The obstacle:** why don't they just…? List the easy exits and plug them.
- **Don'ts are never obstacles.**

## Check 3 — curve placement

Each strand rides its declared fortune curve; every beat lands where the curve says. Cinderella's crash is obligatory; a man in a hole must climb out. **Stagger** — never all strands dipping in the same chapter.

## Check 4 — braid compliance (ensembles)

The declared mechanisms govern: the asset never carries plot; each strand answers the chapter question its own way; the circulating object moves; every strand keeps a relationship line in motion. Strands converge only at declared key events; invented coincidences spend credibility.

## Check 5 — escalation and the gun ledger

Obstacles compound **serially** — rocks, not rotation; each worse than the last. Nothing pays off unplanted; nothing planted goes unused — update the card's gun ledger on every plant and payoff. The character **visibly tries** every unit. Inciting action by chapter 2.

## Check 6 — stakes legible on the page

A reader opening the chapter cold gets why it matters from the panels and balloons. Captions may frame, never carry. If the stakes only exist in a caption's explanation, redraw the beat so the want and the obstacle are visible in what characters do.

## How results show up

In a chapter outline, the checks shape the list of concrete scenes; they don't appear as craft commentary. Flag a failure in one line only when the author has to decide something.

## Changing the frame

A failed check is sometimes the frame's fault. Changing a declared curve, mechanism or the story contract is a `story.md` decision — take it to the author and log it in `kickoff-state.md`; never bend it quietly.
